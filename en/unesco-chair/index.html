UNESCO Chair

Bringing cultural safeguarding and future-making into one shared learning endeavour.

Shiga University has decided to establish the UNESCO Chair on STEAM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts/Liberal arts, and Mathematics) Education for Braiding Traditional Cultural Resources with Science.

The Chair develops education that “braids” traditional cultural resources with scientific inquiry without reducing one knowledge system to the other. It connects education, culture, and science, technology and innovation, seeking to safeguard cultural resources while enabling sustainable and responsible ways of shaping the future.

What the UNESCO Chair seeks to achieve

1｜STEAM education that connects cultural resources and science

Materials, food, architecture, agriculture, craft and everyday cultural practices are not decorative examples added to a science lesson. Together with knowledge holders and communities, we co-design learning that respects history, environment, technology and values as integral parts of inquiry.

2｜Sustainable and responsible innovation that safeguards culture

The aim is not simply to extract traditional knowledge for new products or technologies. We ask whose knowledge is being used, under what conditions it may be shared, how decisions are made, and how benefits and outputs are returned. Learning is connected with contemporary challenges including climate action, water management, plastic pollution and disaster resilience.

3｜Teachers and learners as agents of curriculum change

Through professional learning, teaching resources, lesson plans, assessment rubrics and MOOCs, the Chair supports teachers in responsibly connecting cultural resources with science. Learners participate not only as recipients of knowledge, but as people who formulate questions, engage with communities and develop proposals for the future.

Two flagship projects

Kakishibu｜From a traditional material to green innovation

Kakishibu is a biodegradable, water-repellent material made by fermenting and ageing the juice of astringent persimmons. Working with producers, craftspeople, scientists, educators, companies and communities, we connect Kakishibu with STEAM learning and international research on plastic alternatives, climate action and marine environments.

Children observe the material, investigate its properties and imagine environmentally responsible uses. The purpose is not simply to preserve an “old” material, but to co-create future possibilities while respecting the knowledge and relationships that sustain it.

Tea｜Shared living heritage as a pathway to inclusive learning

Tea brings together botany, cultivation, processing, extraction, aroma, taste, utensils, social practices, trade and community memory. The “Tea Workshop—Tastes, Traditions and Shared Intangible Cultural Heritage,” developed by the Kano Lab and collaborators, involved high-school students and deaf and hard-of-hearing primary-school students.

The lesson connected language, biology, chemistry, history, geography and home economics. Through comparison and dialogue, learners were invited not to identify a single correct taste, but to express sensory differences and explore both diversity and shared cultural connections. The project is featured in a UNESCO report and official story on bringing East Asia’s living heritage into classrooms.

Core activities

Co-design and implementation of STEAM programmes

Knowledge holders, educators, scientists, students and cultural organisations work together across classrooms, universities, communities, museums and online settings. Alongside Kakishibu and tea, future examples include rice cultivation and sake brewing, and traditional architecture, linked with climate action, water management and disaster resilience.

Teacher learning and institutional capacity development

Professional learning for pre-service and in-service teachers combines hands-on inquiry, collaborative curriculum design, reflection and follow-up support. Teaching materials, lesson plans, assessment tools and MOOCs will be shared in forms that can be adapted to local contexts.

Community and civil-society co-creation

Farmers, producers, craftspeople and other cultural knowledge holders are recognised as co-creators rather than sources of information. Attribution, authorship, the use of images and narratives, intellectual property and the return of benefits are discussed before activities begin.

Research, publication and international mobility

Mixed-methods research examines learning, teacher agency and co-design processes. Findings are shared through academic publications, books, educational resources and accessible public outputs. Student exchanges, visiting fellowships, collaborative research, conferences and seminars connect locally grounded knowledge with international dialogue.

Ways to participate and collaborate

The UNESCO Chair welcomes conversations about:

  • co-designing STEAM lessons and resources based on traditional cultural resources;
  • teacher learning, MOOCs and school- or community-based implementation;
  • PhD study, JSPS fellowships and visiting research;
  • student and researcher mobility and comparative studies;
  • co-creation with knowledge holders, cultural organisations, public bodies and companies; and
  • international conferences, seminars and public programmes.
