Responsible AI in Education

Before introducing AI, ask whose learning it should support and according to which values.

Generative AI and educational technology may transform lesson preparation, learning support, assessment and administration. They also raise new questions about educational purpose, teacher expertise, learner autonomy, data, equity and accountability.

The Kano Lab asks not only whether a tool works or saves time, but whether it should be used, who should decide, and how benefits and risks are distributed.

Teachers and AI｜Keeping professional agency at the centre

Teachers should not become people who simply implement AI outputs. Their ability to interpret curricula, understand learners, exercise judgement and choose not to use AI must be respected.

We study how AI reorganises teachers’ work, whether decisions can be explained, and how technology affects relationships between teachers and learners.

Educational data and governance

Educational data may include learning histories, behaviour, assessment and personal attributes with long-term implications. Institutions should not collect everything that can technically be collected. Purpose, data minimisation, retention, access, third-party sharing, deletion and routes for contesting decisions must be designed.

We examine responsibilities among schools, education authorities, universities, companies, families and learners.

ELSI and RRI

Ethical, legal and social issues and responsible research and innovation should not be checklists added after harm occurs. They should shape development, procurement, introduction, professional learning, evaluation and review.

Relevant issues include privacy, intellectual property, bias, transparency, explainability, responsibility, children’s rights, accessibility and environmental impacts.

Inclusion and equity

AI designed for an “average” learner may disadvantage people across disability, language, culture, gender and socioeconomic circumstances. We investigate possibilities for support technologies while asking how to avoid new forms of exclusion, surveillance and dependency.

Key research questions

  • How should roles between teachers and AI be designed around educational purpose rather than efficiency alone?
  • How can teachers and learners challenge an AI-supported decision?
  • What should schools examine when procuring educational data or AI services?
  • How does generative AI affect assessment, authorship, creativity and learner self-efficacy?
  • How can AI support deaf and hard-of-hearing or multilingual learners without creating additional risks?
  • How can educators, government, companies, researchers and the public co-design responsible introduction?

Research opportunities

We welcome proposals from education, AI ethics, information studies, law, policy, data governance, teacher education, disability studies and design. Proposals should not assume that a particular technology must be introduced; begin with educational purpose and the people affected.
