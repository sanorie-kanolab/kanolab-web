Pluripotent Stem Cell Education: iPS and ES Cells

Learning frontier life science through experiments, ethics and the work of researchers.

Knowing the terminology and achievements of iPS and ES cell research is not enough to understand how science works. The iCeMS/CiRA Classroom, Try Stem Cell Research!, was developed so that upper-secondary students could encounter pluripotent stem-cell observation and experiments, hypothesis testing, uncertainty, ethical and social issues, and the work of researchers.

Aims of the laboratory programme

  • Understand pluripotency, self-renewal and differentiation through observation and experimentation.
  • Experience the cycle of hypothesising, testing and revising an explanation.
  • Speak with researchers about everyday work, collaboration, failure, funding and ethics.
  • Distinguish questions that science can address from questions requiring value judgements.
  • Consider frontier research through multiple perspectives rather than expectation or anxiety alone.

Science, ethics and society

Pluripotent stem-cell research raises questions about sources of cells, informed consent, safety in clinical application, equitable access and research reporting, as well as hopes for medicine.

The programme connected scientific knowledge with knowledge about science itself. Students discussed questions that cannot be resolved by scientific evidence alone.

Development into a game

A board-game activity used in the laboratory programme was developed into the educational game Try Stem Cell Research! Players take the role of researchers and experience not only cell differentiation but research funding, conferences, failed experiments and collaboration.
