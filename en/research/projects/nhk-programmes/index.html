NHK Educational Television: Think Like a Crow and Viewpoint Science

Bringing scientific ways of seeing and thinking to television and learning environments.

Kei Kano served on the programme committees of NHK Educational TV’s Think Like a Crow—The Scientific Method and Viewpoint Science, contributing to programme production, development and research support.

The lab’s role was not limited to using finished programmes in workshops. Together with production partners, we considered how questions and images could encourage viewers to observe, compare, predict, hypothesise and test. These ideas were then extended into classroom teaching, hands-on activities and educational resources.

Commissioned research underpinning the programmes

Before the programmes were produced, the lab led two commissioned research projects for NHK on how content could communicate science not simply as settled knowledge, but as a process involving hypotheses, observation, comparison, testing, interpretation, revision and uncertainty.

In FY2012, the team reviewed cases in Japan and abroad and identified design principles for communicating scientific processes and uncertainty. In FY2013, those findings were translated into interactive content and tested in practice, examining how viewers could predict, investigate and revise their thinking. Together, the two studies provided a research foundation for the approaches later developed through Think Like a Crow and Viewpoint Science.

Think Like a Crow—The Scientific Method

The programme did not rush to explain familiar phenomena. It invited viewers to begin with “What if…?”, make a hypothesis, observe, test and revise their thinking in light of the result.

Kei Kano served as a programme committee member in the 2013 academic year and developed research on primary-school lessons, programme-linked workshops and active learning. Videos based on hands-on STEM workshops such as “grains of sesame and salt,” “a drone and a scale,” and “a tray and balloons” remain available online.

Viewpoint Science

This programme investigated one object through multiple “viewpoints”: comparing, counting, arranging, separating and looking across time.

Kei Kano served as a programme committee member from the 2017 to 2018 academic years and contributed to production, development and research support. Its approach later informed a book for homes and schools and The Comparing Game, which invites players to generate questions, compare and classify.

Explore Viewpoint Science on NHK for School

From television to classrooms, workshops and resources

Television can be the beginning rather than the end of learning.

  • Give learners time to predict before revealing a result.
  • Revisit the same object through different viewpoints.
  • Express an idea through words, diagrams and movement.
  • Compare predictions and revise an explanation.
  • Value the shared process of questioning and testing, not only agreement on an answer.
